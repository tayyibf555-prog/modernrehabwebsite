# PRODUCT.md — Modern Rehab (impeccable variant)

## Register
**brand** — this is a marketing/landing surface. The site IS the product; a visitor's impression decides whether they book.

## Product purpose
Modern Rehab is a one-practitioner chronic-pain and injury clinic in Cathcart, Glasgow, run by James McCaig (BSc Hons Sports Therapy, 12+ years private practice, Society of Sports Therapists). The site converts cold visitors into booked Pain Strategy sessions (£99 / 60 min) and downstream programmes (4-week £379, 8-week £699).

The competitor set is not other slick clinics. It's the failed alternatives the visitor has already tried: NHS physio with a paper handout, painkillers, scans that found nothing actionable, surgery consultations, repeat osteopath visits, YouTube stretches. The site has to feel like a different kind of answer.

## Users

### Primary: "Six-years-deep"
- 35–60. Roughly 60% women, 40% men.
- Lives with chronic pain that has resisted at least two prior interventions (NHS physio, GP referrals, injections, sometimes surgery).
- Researching at night, on a phone, often in bed. Pain is recent in their head — flare-up, a bad day, just saw a specialist who shrugged.
- Skeptical. Has been to clinics with friendly fonts and seven framed certificates that did nothing.
- Wants to know: does this person know what they're doing, will they actually examine me, and is there a real plan — not "let's see how it feels next week."

### Secondary: "The athlete with a stubborn injury"
- 28–55, runs / lifts / cycles. Injury is gating their training.
- Has tried sports physio. Wants strength-rehab, not "rest and ice".
- Reads `Trusted & trained at` credentials. Cares about evidence base.

### Tertiary: "Family referral"
- Sent a link by someone whose pain James fixed.
- Already half-sold; just needs to find the booking flow.

## Brand voice
- **Direct.** No softening. "Sick of pills, scans, and rest that never works."
- **Muscular.** The work is physical — manual therapy, strength rehab, deadlifts. Don't ever euphemise.
- **Considered.** Twelve years of cases informs every claim. No buzzwords ("holistic", "wellness journey", "mind-body"). No emoji.
- **Glasgow.** Working-class, plain-speaking, slightly dry. Not Mayfair-shiny, not American-rah-rah.
- **Quietly confident.** James doesn't need to shout. The work does the talking. The site reflects that.

Avoid: anything that reads as wellness-spa, holistic, "journey", "wellness", "self-care", "your healing path." Avoid the soft humanist clinic register entirely.

## Three brand-voice words (physical)
**Direct. Muscular. Considered.**

Physical objects this brand resembles:
- A serious training log, leather-bound, with handwritten numbers and dates.
- The signage above a Glasgow boxing gym that's been repainted properly over the years.
- A British sports-therapy textbook from the late 1980s — clinical photographs, two-column setting, no design-thinking.
- A wood-and-leather chiropractor's table, well-used.

NOT: spa, lavender, sunrise, illustrated mascots, abstract gradients, watercolor, friendly-physio.

## Anti-references (do NOT design like)
- **Bupa / Vitality / private health corporate.** Stock photography, navy + teal, "your wellness partner".
- **Boutique London wellness clinics.** Cream, ivory, gold sans-serif, candle aesthetic.
- **Calm.com / Headspace.** Soft round illustrations, generic encouragement.
- **Local-physio template sites.** Wix templates with stethoscope icons.
- **SaaS landing pages.** Hero-with-screenshot, "Trusted by", three-feature grid, "Get started free."
- **Editorial-magazine aesthetic.** Display-serif italic + small mono labels + ruled separators. (See impeccable's reflex-reject lanes list.) Avoid.

## Anchor references (DO learn from)
1. **Whoop.com** — dark UI carrying biometric authority for a health brand. Clinical confidence without spa softness.
2. **Frank Netter, "Atlas of Human Anatomy" plates.** Clinical anatomical reference as design language for the body-map UI. Plates, not cartoons.
3. **Strava year-in-review pages.** Body-data made personal and considered, without being twee.

## Strategic principles
1. **The body is the navigation.** A pain clinic for chronic-pain visitors should ask "where does it hurt?" with an anatomical body, not a row of chips. Make this the page's signature interaction.
2. **Orange is the brand.** #E67E2A is load-bearing, not an accent. The page commits to it — drenched sections, not a 10% sprinkle.
3. **No template moves.** No hero-metric grid (12+ / 3,090 / 8–16 / 1-on-1). No "Trusted at" marquee. No card grid of identical service tiles. No "/ 01 — Approach" magazine numbering. Each section earns its shape.
4. **The result is the offer.** Real cases (Ian / Audrey / Dawn) and real testimonials carry the page. Pricing is secondary; clinical trust is primary.
5. **Conversion is the booking flow.** Every CTA points at one thing: book a Pain Strategy session. No newsletter. No "learn more about James."

## Constraints
- Real practitioner, real clinic, real testimonials — copy is non-fiction. Don't invent metrics, don't paraphrase testimonials into prettier shapes.
- UK-only audience. £ pricing, UK English, GMT/BST times. 24h cancellation policy is hard.
- Performance-aware: most visitors are on phone, on 4G, at night. Heavy animations and unoptimized photos hurt conversion.
- The booking flow must actually work end-to-end (calendar → time → details → confirmation). Mock data is fine but the UX is real.

## Success
- The visitor finishes the page knowing: this is one person, twelve years of experience, treats causes not symptoms, has documented cases, books here.
- The visitor doesn't think "another clinic site"; they think "this is unusual" or "this looks like it knows what it's doing."
- The body-map interaction is something they'd describe to a friend.
